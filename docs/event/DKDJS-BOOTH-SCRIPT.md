# Castle Gardens, Saturday 17 October — booth plan

10am–4pm · The Castle Gardens, Kuna · free and open to the public · ~36 exhibitors

---

## The one thing that decides whether the day was worth it

You will have thirty to sixty conversations. **The only ones that matter are the ones where a date ends up on the sheet.** Everything below exists to get to that point.

A good conversation that ends with "great, check out our website" is a lost lead. They won't.

Target: **15 dates on paper.** That is a good day. Five is a poor day regardless of how fun it was.

---

## The twenty-second answer

When someone asks what you do:

> "We're Daniel and Kathy — we're a husband-and-wife DJ and MC team. Both of us come to every event, and we only take one a day. Are you planning something?"

Three things in one breath: who, what's different, and a question back. The question is the important part — it hands them the next move and gets you off the pitch.

**Don't lead with equipment.** Nobody books a DJ because of a speaker model. Lead with the two of you, because that's the thing nobody else at the event can say.

---

## The follow-up, depending on what they say

**"I'm getting married."**
> "Congratulations — do you have a date yet?"

If yes, you're already at the sheet. If no:
> "When you do, come back to us. We only take one event a day, so dates go early."

**"I'm just looking."**
> "Fair enough. Put your date down anyway and we'll tell you if it's open — costs you nothing and it's useful to know."

**"How much do you charge?"**
This is your moment. Most of the room will dodge this question.
> "Weddings start at $1,095 for four hours, and every price is published on our website. You can see the whole list before you ever talk to us."

Then show the sheet or the sign. The point is not the number, it's that you answered.

**"What makes you different?"**
> "Two of us at every event, and we only take one a day. While one of us runs music and the microphone, the other can chase down the maid of honour who's wandered off with the toast."

That line gets a laugh and it's true, which is why it works.

**"Do you do [karaoke / corporate / line dancing]?"**
Yes to all three. Karaoke is $450, corporate is $895. Kathy line dances weekly — that one is genuinely yours.

---

## The ask

Do not wait for the end of the conversation. The moment they mention a date:

> "Let me grab that date and we'll check it for you."

Then hand them the pen. People fill in a form that is already in front of them far more readily than one they're invited to find.

**If they hesitate:** "Just the date and a phone number is fine."

---

## The part most people at this event will miss

**Thirty-five other vendors are in that room, and they matter more than most of the couples.**

Photographers, planners, florists, caterers and venues all get asked "do you know a good DJ?" — constantly, and by people who have already decided to spend money. One planner who likes you is worth more than twenty brochures handed to browsers.

Two venues are exhibiting: **Rooney Ranch & Club** and **Blazek Event Center**. Both are exactly the kind of preferred-vendor relationship that would help you more than anything else you could do this year.

Make time to walk the floor. The line is simple:

> "We're the DJs at booth [x]. Do you keep a preferred vendor list? We'd love to be on it — and happy to send people your way too."

That is the single highest-value thing you can do on Saturday, and it costs nothing but twenty minutes.

**Collect their cards.** Follow up Monday.

      The LED booth does not arrive until late November, so this is the only moving

- [ ] Tent weights on all four legs. A 10x10 in open ground is a sail.
- [ ] Clipboards or binder clips. Loose paper outdoors lasts about four minutes.
- [ ] One pink thing. Black tent, black spandex, black speakers — without it you are
      a black tent in a field rather than DKDJS.

## Setting the tent up

**Do not put the table across the front.** It turns a 10x10 into a counter people
talk at instead of a space they walk into. Put it along one side or at the back,
leave the front open, and point the two director chairs inward. Most booths cannot
offer somebody a seat — a seated conversation runs five minutes instead of forty
seconds, and five minutes is where dates get written down.

Low background volume is the right call and it is also a demonstration. Music people
can talk over is the actual skill. If the vendor next door can still hold a
conversation, you are doing the job — worth saying out loud to anyone who mentions it.

---

## Careful what you promise about the LED booth

The booth ships from China and does not arrive until mid-to-late November. Both
**The Full Day** and **The Whole Night** bundle it, and it is a $349 add-on
everywhere else.

Weddings booked on Saturday will be 2027 dates, so they are fine. The risk is a
November or early-December holiday party or karaoke night — a date inside the gap.

Rule for the day: **do not promise the booth for any date before 1 December**, and
do not promise it at all until the crate is open and it has been powered up.
Shipping estimates slip and customs does not care about your calendar. If someone
wants a near-term date, either sell it without the booth or say plainly that it is
arriving and you will confirm nearer the time.

## One thing to decide before Saturday

People will ask **"when will you get back to me?"** You have never settled a response-time promise, and the website doesn't make one.

Pick a number you can actually hit and say the same thing all day. "By Monday" is honest and easy to keep for a Saturday event. Do not say "within an hour" on a day you'll be standing in a field until four.

---

## Monday morning

This is where most expo leads die.

1. **Check every date on the sheet.** Add anything booked to the Bookings collection.
2. **Reply to all of them** — including, and especially, the ones whose date is taken. "Your date is gone, but here's who else is good" earns more goodwill than silence, and people remember it.
3. **Email every vendor whose card you took.** Short: good to meet you, here's our site, happy to recommend you.
4. **Ask the organisers for the attendee list** if they offer one.
5. **Post photos** from the day while it's still current.

---

## What to notice on the day

Worth jotting down between conversations:

- Which question people asked first
- Whether anyone recognised DKDJS already
- What other DJs were charging and how they answered the price question
- Which bit of your pitch made people lean in

That's free market research you cannot get any other way, and it's gone by Tuesday if you don't write it down.
