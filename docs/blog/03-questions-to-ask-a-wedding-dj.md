# 12 Questions to Ask a Wedding DJ Before You Book

Most couples hire a wedding DJ once. The companies you are calling do this every weekend, which means the conversation is uneven — you do not yet know which questions separate a good outcome from a bad one.

These twelve do. For each, the answer you want and why it matters.

## 1. Is my date available, and will you hold it?

**What you want:** a straight yes or no, and a clear statement of what it takes to hold it — usually a signed agreement plus a retainer.

**Why it matters:** this is the only question with a deadline attached. Ask it first, before you invest an hour in a conversation about a date that is already gone.

## 2. Will *you* be the DJ at my wedding?

**What you want:** the name of the person who will actually be there, in writing.

**Why it matters:** larger companies assign whoever is free. You can have an excellent meeting with a company owner and meet a stranger on the day. This is the single most common source of wedding-DJ disappointment, and it is entirely avoidable by asking.

## 3. How many events do you take per day?

**What you want:** one, ideally.

**Why it matters:** a DJ with an afternoon event across town is watching a clock that has nothing to do with your timeline. Setup gets rushed, teardown starts early, and if the first event runs long, yours absorbs it.

## 4. Do you MC, or only play music?

**What you want:** clear confirmation that announcements are included, not an add-on.

**Why it matters:** somebody has to introduce the wedding party, cue the first dance, tell two hundred people that dinner is served, and hand the microphone to a nervous best man. If it is not the DJ, it defaults to your venue coordinator, your maid of honour, or nobody. "Nobody" is what produces a reception where guests drift out at nine.

## 5. How many hours does the package cover, and what happens if we run over?

**What you want:** the number of hours and the overtime rate, stated up front.

**Why it matters:** hours are the main driver of price, and the most common place a quote turns out not to mean what you assumed. Ask specifically whether setup and teardown come out of your hours.

## 6. Is the ceremony included?

**What you want:** a direct answer, because a ceremony is a separate sound system in a separate place.

**Why it matters:** outdoor ceremonies in the Treasure Valley are common and wind is real. An officiant microphone and a second system are not luxuries if your vows happen outside — they are the difference between guests hearing the ceremony and watching it.

## 7. What backup equipment travels with you?

**What you want:** spare microphones, spare cables, and redundancy on anything critical.

**Why it matters:** equipment fails. It is not a hypothetical, it is a matter of frequency. The question is whether the failure costs you thirty seconds or thirty minutes.

## 8. Can we give you a do-not-play list?

**What you want:** an enthusiastic yes, and a process for collecting it.

**Why it matters:** a DJ who resists this is telling you they intend to run their show rather than yours. A good one will have a music questionnaire with space for both the must-plays and the never-plays, and will hold to it.

## 9. How do you handle guest requests?

**What you want:** a described method — filtered against your lists, read against the room.

**Why it matters:** "we play whatever guests ask for" and "we ignore all requests" are both wrong answers. You want someone who will take the request, check it against your do-not-play list, and judge whether it fits the moment.

## 10. What do you need from the venue, and have you worked there?

**What you want:** power requirements, space, load-in access, and an honest answer about familiarity.

**Why it matters:** "I have not worked there but I will call them" is a perfectly good answer. "I do not need to know" is not. Venue logistics are where day-of problems originate.

## 11. What is the total, and what is the payment schedule?

**What you want:** the full number including every add-on and any travel charge, plus retainer amount and balance due date.

**Why it matters:** travel fees in particular are easy to miss. A quote that is competitive for a Boise venue can change considerably for a mountain one. Get the total for *your* venue, not the starting price.

## 12. What happens if you cannot make it?

**What you want:** a real answer — a named backup, a network, or a refund provision — rather than reassurance.

**Why it matters:** it is the question nobody asks and the one with the worst downside. You are looking for evidence that the company has thought about it before you brought it up.

## How to use the answers

Ask all twelve of the two or three companies on your shortlist, in the same order, and write the answers down. The pattern matters more than any single response: a company that answers 2, 3 and 12 without hedging is generally a company that answers everything else well too.

And notice what you are not being told. Vagueness about who shows up, how many hours you get, or what the total is tends not to resolve itself later.
