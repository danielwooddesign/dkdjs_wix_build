# What a Wedding DJ Costs in Boise, Idaho

The honest answer is somewhere between $400 and $2,500, which is not much help when you are building a budget. So here is the actual breakdown — what the number is made of, what pushes it up, and where to draw your line.

## The benchmark

The average Idaho wedding in 2026 runs about $26,000, and entertainment accounts for roughly 4% of that — about **$1,100**. That figure is a useful starting point and a slightly misleading one, because "entertainment" covers everything from a four-hour reception DJ to a full-day setup with ceremony audio, and those are not the same purchase.

Treat $1,100 as the middle of the market, not the price of a specific thing.

## What you are actually paying for

The confusing part of DJ pricing is that two quotes can differ by a thousand dollars and both be fair, because they are for different amounts of work. Five things account for almost all of the variation.

**Hours of coverage.** The single biggest factor. A four-hour reception is a different job from an eight-hour day that starts with a ceremony at four and ends with a send-off at eleven. Most DJs price in tiers by hours and sell extra hours à la carte after that.

**Whether the ceremony is included.** A ceremony is a second sound system in a second location, usually outdoors, with a microphone on an officiant who will not be miked again. It gets set up, sound-checked, run, and then struck while cocktail hour is happening. It is genuinely a second event, and it is priced like one.

**How many people show up.** Many DJ companies send one person. Some send two. This is the part of a quote that is hardest to compare, because a solo DJ physically cannot be at the microphone and also be finding your missing maid of honour, and the gap shows up during the parts of the night that matter most.

**Lighting and visual add-ons.** Dance-floor lighting is usually standard. Uplighting around the room, a monogram on the wall, an LED booth or video wall — these are extras, typically $200 to $600 each.

**Travel.** Read this line carefully in any quote. Some Treasure Valley DJs include travel within a set radius and bill beyond it; mountain venues like McCall or Sun Valley can add several hundred dollars or more. A quote that looks cheap in Boise may not be cheap in Garden Valley.

## What a low number usually means

There are good DJs working at the bottom of the market, and there are reasons a quote comes in at $500 that have nothing to do with skill. But when a wedding quote is dramatically under the local range, it is usually one of these:

- Fewer hours than you think, with the overage billed at a high hourly rate
- One person, doing sound and MC and troubleshooting simultaneously
- No backup equipment, which is fine until a microphone dies during the toasts
- Music only, with no MC — meaning nobody announces the wedding party, cues the first dance, or tells two hundred people that dinner is served
- Travel, setup, or teardown billed separately
- A part-time DJ who has another wedding the same day

None of those are disqualifying on their own. They just need to be on the table before you compare two numbers and conclude one is better value.

## The retainer question

Nearly every DJ takes a deposit — more properly a retainer — to hold your date, and it is almost always non-refundable, because holding your date means turning other couples away for it. Local retainers commonly run from a couple of hundred dollars up to $500 or more.

Ours is a flat **$200**, non-refundable, with the balance due 14 days before the event. Ask what the number is and when the rest is due before you sign anything; the answer tells you how the company runs.

## How to set your own number

Work backwards from coverage, not from a target price.

Write down the time your ceremony starts and the time you want the last song to play. That span, plus setup, is what you are buying. Decide whether you need ceremony audio. Decide whether you want one person or two in the room. Then compare quotes that cover the same hours with the same staffing — and only then look at the add-ons.

Most couples who do this find the real decision is between two packages, not between six companies.
