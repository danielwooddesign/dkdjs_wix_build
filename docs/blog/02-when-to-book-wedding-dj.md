# When to Book Your Wedding DJ in the Treasure Valley

Short answer: **nine to twelve months out** for a Saturday between June and October. Six months is usually workable outside peak season. Inside ninety days you are not out of luck, but your choices narrow fast.

Here is why the timing works the way it does, and what to do if you are already later than you wanted to be.

## A DJ is not like most of your other vendors

Most wedding vendors can stretch. A florist can take three weddings in a weekend. A bakery can bake more cakes. A caterer can add staff.

A DJ cannot be in two places at once. Availability is binary — a date is open or it is gone — and that is true of every DJ you will contact, regardless of how big the company is. Multi-DJ companies get around it by sending whoever is free, which means the person you met at the consultation may not be the person at your reception. Ask about that specifically.

We only take one event per day, so when we say a date is open, it is open for both of us, for the whole day, and we are not splitting attention with a second wedding across town.

## How Treasure Valley dates actually go

Wedding season here is shaped by the weather, and it is narrower than in a lot of the country. The valley gets busy in **June** and stays busy through **early October**, with September in particular getting heavy — the light is good, the heat has broken, and every outdoor venue in Eagle and Boise is booked.

Within that window, dates disappear in a predictable order:

1. **Saturdays in September and June.** These go first, often a year or more ahead.
2. **Saturdays elsewhere in peak season.** July and August, then early October.
3. **Holiday weekends.** Memorial Day and Labor Day, for the extra travel day.
4. **Fridays in peak season.** Increasingly popular and increasingly not a bargain.
5. **Everything else.** Sundays, and anything from November through April.

If your date is in that last group, you have more room than the internet will tell you. A February wedding booked four months out is usually fine.

## The order to book in

Vendors are not equally urgent, and booking them out of order wastes money and time.

**First: venue and date.** Everything else depends on this. Do not contact a single other vendor until the date is signed.

**Second: photographer and DJ.** These two are the classic bottleneck. Both are one-per-day businesses, both book far ahead, and both are hard to replace at short notice. Book them in the same week if you can.

**Third: caterer, florist, cake, officiant.** These have more capacity and more flexibility.

**Last: the details.** Transport, favours, day-of rentals, the stuff that can be sorted in the final two months.

Couples who book the DJ fifth or sixth routinely find the good ones gone and end up choosing from whoever is left — which is a bad way to pick the person running your timeline.

## What "booked" actually means

A conversation is not a booking. A quote is not a booking. An email saying "we'd love to have you" is not a booking.

A date is held when there is a signed agreement and a retainer paid. Until then, the DJ is free to take another couple for that date, and a good one will tell you so plainly if someone else asks. Ours is a flat $200 retainer with the balance due 14 days before the event, and the date is not held until it is paid.

If a company is vague about what it takes to secure a date, that is worth noticing.

## If your date is close

Inside ninety days, work differently.

**Lead with the date, not the questions.** Send a short message that opens with the date, the venue and the hours you need. Most DJs will answer an availability question in a day; a long questionnaire slows everyone down.

**Contact several at once.** In normal circumstances it is worth having a careful conversation with each. At short notice, availability filters the list for you.

**Be flexible on package, firm on coverage.** You can usually drop uplighting or a monogram. Do not drop the hours you actually need, and do not drop ceremony audio to save a few hundred dollars if you are getting married outdoors.

**Expect the same price.** Last-minute does not mean discounted. A DJ holding an open Saturday in September has no reason to cut the rate, and a DJ who does cut it steeply may be telling you why the date is still open.

## One more thing worth knowing

The date you want is not equally scarce for every vendor, which means the phrase "we're getting close" means different things to different people. A caterer saying you are late and a DJ saying you are late are describing different problems. The DJ one is harder to solve.

If you have a date, the single most useful thing you can do this week is find out who is free.
