# Booking a DJ for Your Company Holiday Party in Boise

If you are the person who got handed the holiday party this year, here is the short version: December has six usable party nights, and they are going.

Everything below is the longer version, including what it costs.

## Six nights. That is the whole season.

Count them. Friday the 4th, Saturday the 5th, Friday the 11th, Saturday the 12th, Friday the 18th, Saturday the 19th. The 25th is Christmas Day, and nobody is holding a company party the weekend after it.

That is the entire December calendar, and every business in the Treasure Valley is trying to book into the same six evenings. The venues that hold sixty to two hundred people are a short list, and they go first. Then the caterer. Then whoever is left.

Entertainment is the piece most often left until last, and it is the one with the least flexibility. A caterer can take two events in a night. A DJ cannot be in two places at once, so by late November the dates still open are the ones nobody wanted.

If your date is already set, the useful thing to do today is find out who is still free for it. If it is not set, work backwards from whichever of the six you can actually get.

## A company party is not a wedding

This matters more than it sounds, and it is where a lot of office parties go quietly wrong.

A wedding has a script. Everyone in the room knows roughly what happens and in what order. A company party has none of that. People arrive from work, stand near the people they already sit near, eat, and wait to find out whether they are allowed to leave.

What actually makes it work is the part nobody budgets for: somebody running the room. That means music at a level people can talk over while they eat, an actual introduction when the boss speaks, a microphone that works for the awards, and a clear moment when the night turns from dinner into something else. Without that, the dance floor stays empty and everyone is in the car park by nine.

That is MC work, and it is included in every package we sell. It is also the single biggest difference between a party people mention in January and one they do not.

## What to sort out before you get quotes

**Speeches and awards.** Almost every company party has a moment where someone talks. Say so up front — it changes the microphone setup and it changes the run of the evening. Handing a nervous regional manager a live mic with nobody standing next to them is how you lose ten minutes.

**The two halves of the night.** Dinner and dancing need different volumes and different music, and the switch between them has to be deliberate. Ask any DJ you talk to how they handle that transition. A vague answer tells you something.

**The crowd is not one crowd.** A company party spans everyone from the twenty-two-year-old in support to someone who has worked there thirty years. That is a much wider range than a wedding, and it is a reading problem rather than a playlist problem.

**Your venue's limits.** Ask about power, load-in access, and what time music has to stop. Venues almost always have a curfew and it is almost always earlier than people expect.

**Who actually decides.** If the date needs sign-off from someone above you, find that out now rather than after you have held a room for a week. In a six-night season, a slow internal approval is how companies end up with a Tuesday.

## If you have left it late

You have not necessarily missed it, but work differently.

**Lead with the date.** Send a short message that opens with the date, the venue and the hours you need. Most DJs will answer an availability question the same day; a long questionnaire slows everyone down.

**Ask several at once.** Availability will filter the list for you faster than comparing quotes will.

**Consider a Thursday.** Nobody wants to hear it, but a Thursday in mid-December is a real party with a far better choice of venue, caterer and entertainment than the Saturday everyone fought over. Attendance holds up better than people expect.

**Expect the same price.** Last-minute does not mean discounted. Anyone holding an open December Saturday has no reason to cut their rate, and one who cuts it steeply may be telling you why the date is still free.

## The honest summary

Most company holiday parties are not ruined by bad music. They are ruined by nobody being in charge of the evening — dinner runs long, the speeches happen over a hum of conversation, the dance floor never opens, and people drift off.

Pick one of the six nights while you still have a choice. Tell whoever you hire what the evening actually contains. And make sure somebody is holding the microphone who knows what to do with it.
